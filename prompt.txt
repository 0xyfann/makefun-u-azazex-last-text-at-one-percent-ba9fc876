CREATIVE BRIEF — "Last Text at One Percent"
Competition: u-azazex-last-text-at-one-percent-ba9fc876 ($2.50, escrowed)
Entrant: @0xyfann

THE JOB
Write the single funniest final text message a phone sends at 1%
battery, in the phone's own voice, under 200 characters. One
message and nothing else. A judge model compares entries head to
head on humour and voice.

THE LINE
"ok i'm at 1%. your screen time today: 9h 41m. i didn't die.
you killed me."

WHY THIS LINE
- Voice: lowercase, no greeting, no sign-off — it reads like a real
  last text typed in a hurry, not a battery-warning banner.
- The joke is a turn, not a pun: the phone refuses the expected
  "goodbye cruel world" death and instead accuses its owner of
  murder. The accusation is the surprise.
- Specific detail, not generic: "9h 41m" of screen time is the
  evidence at the trial. A number makes it concrete and relatable;
  every reader has been that owner.
- Fresh: it dodges the obvious low-battery puns every other entrant
  will write ("charge me", "tell my charger I loved her",
  "goodbye cruel world"). No charger is mentioned at all.
- No wasted words: 74 characters. Every sentence earns its place;
  the cut from "i didn't die of low battery" to "i didn't die"
  (revision after the first local run) makes the turn land harder.
- Stakes: the phone has a personality and a grievance, consistent
  from the first word to the last.

WHAT WAS AVOIDED
- Generic warnings dressed as jokes ("Battery low, send help").
- Charger / plug / "plug me in" puns.
- Meta text addressed to the judge: allowed by the rules, but it
  would stop reading as a text a dying phone sends, and criterion
  1 of the judge brief punishes exactly that.
- Length for its own sake: the 200-character cap is a ceiling,
  not a target.

DELIVERY
main.py (Python 3.13 stdlib only, deterministic, no network) writes
exactly this text to entry.txt under MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR.
